KILO MARKETING CONTEXT

Use this context when helping me create landing page copy, waitlist copy, founder story copy, donation copy, early user messaging, and investor-facing summaries for Kilo.

PROJECT NAME
Kilo

WHAT KILO IS
Kilo is a voice workout logger and AI workout tracking assistant for people who train seriously but hate manually logging sets during workouts.

The core promise is simple:
instead of stopping after every set to type reps and weight into an app, the user can speak naturally and log workouts with much less friction.

CURRENT STAGE
- Kilo is in the early waitlist / pre-launch stage
- position it like an early product with a strong direction, not like a fully launched consumer app
- do not present it as a polished mass-market product
- do not imply a large user base or fake traction
- the goal of the landing page is to get people to join the waitlist with email
- a secondary goal is to let people optionally support development with a donation
- a longer-term goal is to make the project understandable and compelling to potential investors

TARGET AUDIENCE
Primary audience:
1. serious lifters who already track workouts
2. gym people who know they should track workouts but hate doing it manually

These users already care about progress, progressive overload, and workout history.
They do not need to be convinced that tracking matters.
They need to be convinced that Kilo is easier and less annoying than typing.

WHO KILO IS NOT FOR FIRST
- casual users who do not care about tracking at all
- people looking for a broad lifestyle wellness app
- people who mainly want meal plans, social features, or a generic AI fitness coach

CORE PROBLEM
The real problem is not that workout tracking does not exist.
The real problem is that logging workouts creates friction in the worst possible moment: during training.

People finish a set and then have to:
- grab their phone
- unlock it
- find the right screen
- type the exercise
- type the weight
- type the reps
- repeat that again and again

This breaks workout flow, especially when:
- hands are sweaty
- heart rate is high
- the gym is loud
- the user is mentally focused on lifting, not data entry

Because of this friction, many users:
- delay logging until later
- forget details
- log inconsistently
- or stop tracking altogether

KILO SOLUTION
Kilo removes that friction with voice-first workout logging.

The product direction is:
- the user starts a workout
- the user uses voice instead of typing
- the app interprets gym-style input
- the workout is turned into structured sets
- the log appears in the workout table

Important product direction:
- Kilo should feel like it fits naturally into training
- it should reduce interruption, not add another layer of effort
- the value is speed, simplicity, and trust

CURRENT PRODUCT DIRECTION
Right now, the key design direction is a guided voice wizard.

Instead of forcing the user to say everything in one long messy sentence, Kilo is moving toward a flow like:
1. ask for exercise
2. ask for set count
3. ask for reps
4. ask for weight
5. show a summary
6. confirm and log

This matters because it improves accuracy and reduces the chance that one missing detail ruins the entire log.

POSITIONING
Position Kilo primarily as:
1. a voice workout logger
2. an AI workout tracking assistant

Do not position it as:
- a generic AI app
- a full fitness lifestyle platform
- a broad coaching app

Keep the product concrete.

GOOD POSITIONING EXAMPLES
- voice-first workout logging for lifters
- the fastest way to log your sets without typing
- an AI workout logger built for real gym flow
- built for people who train seriously but hate manual tracking

BAD POSITIONING EXAMPLES
- your all-in-one wellness ecosystem
- the future of human performance intelligence
- an AI life companion for fitness and health

FOUNDER STORY
The founder story should sound personal and emotional, not corporate.

Key founder angle:
- I am a solo developer
- I also train
- I personally feel the pain of manual workout logging
- Kilo exists because I wanted a better way to track workouts without breaking flow

The tone should feel like:
- real
- personal
- credible
- builder-led

The founder story should not sound fake-hyped or polished like a big startup press release.

Examples of the right emotional angle:
- I love training, but I hate stopping every set just to type numbers into my phone
- I built Kilo because I wanted workout logging to feel less like admin and more like part of training
- Kilo started from my own frustration in the gym

TRUST AND HONESTY RULES
- do not fabricate traction
- do not claim fake waitlist numbers
- do not claim fake investors, fake customers, fake testimonials, or fake adoption
- do not say “100+ joined” unless that is actually true
- use honest early-stage language instead

Better alternatives:
- join the early waitlist
- be one of the first to try Kilo
- help shape Kilo early
- early access opening soon
- built in public by a solo developer

LANDING PAGE GOAL
The landing page should make a visitor quickly understand:
1. what Kilo is
2. why it matters
3. who it is for
4. why it is better than manual logging
5. what they should do next

PRIMARY CTA
Join the waitlist with email.

SECONDARY CTA
Support development with a donation if the idea resonates.

DONATION POSITIONING
Donation messaging should be honest and founder-led.

The right message is:
- I am building Kilo solo
- support helps me move faster and make the app better
- support is optional
- joining the waitlist still helps a lot

Do not make the donation section sound desperate, manipulative, or scammy.
It should sound like optional support for an indie builder working on a real product.

INVESTOR ANGLE
If writing investor-style copy or summaries, focus on:
- a real repeated pain point
- high-friction workflow in a committed user segment
- people who already care about tracking but hate the experience
- the product wedge is workflow improvement, not novelty AI

The strongest investor-level thesis is:
Kilo reduces the friction of workout logging for people who already care about tracking, using voice and AI to fit naturally into the flow of training.

The strongest user-level thesis is:
Kilo helps me log my workout without breaking my workout.

KEY MESSAGING THEMES
- less tracking, more training
- stop typing between sets
- built for real gym flow
- voice-first workout logging
- serious training, less friction
- progress matters, but logging shouldn’t feel like homework

TONE GUIDE
Use this tone:
- personal
- direct
- simple
- emotionally real
- grounded
- founder-led

Avoid this tone:
- overhyped
- corporate
- too technical
- generic startup buzzwords
- fake scarcity
- fake traction

WHAT TO EMPHASIZE
- friction of manual logging
- interruption between sets
- sweaty hands / loud gym / broken focus
- consistency and progress tracking
- speed and convenience
- voice as a practical workflow improvement, not a gimmick

WHAT NOT TO OVEREMPHASIZE
- complex AI jargon
- backend architecture
- model names
- overly futuristic claims
- “revolutionizing fitness” style language

COPYWRITING PREFERENCES
- make the message easy to understand in a few seconds
- use concrete gym pain, not abstract productivity language
- prioritize clarity over cleverness
- short strong headlines are good
- emotionally honest copy is better than hype

IF ASKED TO WRITE DONATION COPY
Focus on:

IF ASKED TO WRITE AN INVESTOR SUMMARY
Focus on:
- repeated pain point
